**Nimbus Mk I** is a student mini-rocket I designed and launched with CLES-FACIL, the aerospace club at INSA Lyon.

  <img src="{{ '/assets/images/projects/nimbus_mk_1_logo.webp' | relative_url }}" alt="The Nimbus Mk I mission patch, with the CLES-FACIL name and the 2018 – 2019 campaign dates.">
  <figcaption>The Nimbus Mk I mission patch (CLES-FACIL, 2018 – 2019).</figcaption>
</figure>

I was there myself with [Manuel Thouillot](https://www.linkedin.com/in/manuel-thouillot-740652222/) when we pushed together the launch button on the artillery field of the 1st *Régiment de hussards parachutistes* (RHP), near Tarbes, France, on a sunny day of July 2019. The rocket flew at around 300 meters altitude, and the parachute deployed according to its PCB's embedded program I had written. Unfortunately, the round parachute suffered a "Mae West" malfunction and the rocket crashed into the ground. 

That same day, I also witnessed the launch of the club's main rocket, **[Astreos](https://www.cles-facil.org/fusex/astreos)**, a massive 3.5-meter-long rocket that also crashed and buried itself deep into the ground. We had to spend a few hours digging it out to try to recover the scientific data it had collected during its flight. It was a day of both joy and disappointment, but overall a very rewarding experience. A good reminder that aerospace is a field where things can go wrong, and that even the most carefully designed systems can fail in unexpected ways. 

It was my first real hands-on engineering project outside of software: I learned soldering, PCB design, and embedded programming along the way, and got to see the thing actually fly.
